Data preparation decision

Ratings marked “Not given” are stored as text, not as true null values.

There are no literal nulls in the CSV. I counted 736 “Not given” ratings and kept them missing in the analysis. I don’t replace them with an average because that would create ratings customers never submitted and could distort restaurant averages.

05 / Conclusions & recommendations

What I would take forward

The conclusions keep the original direction while separating what the data shows from what should be tested.

Recommendations

Test weekday incentives.

Reduce or adjust weekday delivery fees in a controlled promotion to encourage orders on slower days. Compare order lift, average order value, and delivery time with a baseline before expanding.

Promote niche restaurants during the week.

Try featuring less-frequented restaurants on weekdays, while making the most popular cuisines easy to find during busy periods. Track whether the placement brings new orders rather than shifting existing ones.

Investigate restaurants with few orders.

There are many restaurants with 20 or fewer orders. Look into discoverability, menu availability, pricing, and customer feedback before deciding which support would help.

Improve rating capture.

Follow up after delivery with a simple rating prompt. Monitor the response rate and the share of low ratings so missing feedback is not mistaken for positive feedback.

How the data is handled

The supplied CSV has 1,898 rows and 9 columns. “Not given” is a missing-rating marker. It remains separate from scores for every rating count, average, and promotion check.

Commission estimate

Applied 25% to order costs above $20 and 15% to order costs above $5 through $20. This is modeled commission, not profit; it excludes other expenses and revenue sources.
